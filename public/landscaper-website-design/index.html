What this page covers

This page is about websites for landscapers. The ones we build put your own photos first, state the towns you work in, and give people a short way to ask for a quote or ring you. They also need to work properly on a phone and be clear enough for Google to understand what you do and where.

If you already get work from referrals or Facebook, keep them. Your own website is the place you own, where the finished work can do the talking.

What matters on a landscaper’s website

Photos of the work

Homeowners decide with their eyes. We use your own finished jobs, grouped by the type of work: pool surrounds, decking, retaining walls, landscaping and paving. Nothing made up to fill the space. You can send the photos through, or we can add placeholders until you do.

The towns you cover

Name the places you actually travel to, once and clearly. That might be Pacific Palms, Forster–Tuncurry, Nabiac and Rainbow Flat, or a different stretch of the Mid North Coast. People need to see quickly that you work where they live.

A quote form people will use

A short form is enough: name, phone, email, suburb, the type of project and a few details. Put your phone number beside it for anyone who’d rather ring. Enquiries can land in your inbox so you can reply when you’re off the tools.

Easy on a phone

Most people will look at your jobs on a phone, often with patchy reception. The pages need to load without a long wait and without the layout jumping around. A heavy site full of large photos costs you enquiries.

Easy to find on Google

Your page titles, service pages and the towns you cover tell Google what you do and where. We set up or tidy your Google Business Profile and link it to the site, then add the search basics: page titles, structured data, a sitemap and Search Console. Nobody can honestly promise you a ranking, and we won’t.

Case study: Dan Amato Landscaping

The brief

Dan builds pool surrounds, retaining walls, decking and complete landscapes between Pacific Palms and Rainbow Flat. His old website named his services and gave a phone number, but gave nobody a way to describe a project and ask for a quote online.

What we built

The first screen says “Let us build your dream outdoor space.” over a photo of a finished pool surround, with two next steps: ask for a quote, or look at the work.

Four photo albums cover pool surrounds, decking, retaining walls, and landscaping and paving, with five photos each. That’s 20 photographs, and every one is a job Dan finished himself.

A six-field quote form asks for a name, phone number, email, suburb, the type of project and a few details. Each enquiry arrives by email, and his phone number sits beside the form.

The site names the four towns he works in. It notes that pool construction is left to the customer’s pool builder, and his ABN and licence sit in the footer.

Key decisions

We let the finished work do the talking, instead of writing a long brochure. Photos are grouped by service, so someone planning a pool surround can go straight to that work. The quote form stays short, and the phone number is right there. The four towns are named once, not stuffed into every sentence.

The outcome

In mobile lab tests the homepage dropped from about 19 MB to 1.7 MB. Performance went from 51 to 73, SEO from 91 to 100 and layout shift from 0.281 to 0.024. It loads faster, which matters when someone is scrolling through job photos on their phone.

In its first two weeks live, Google Search Console shows his site averaging position 3 when people search his business name. We handle his site care.
