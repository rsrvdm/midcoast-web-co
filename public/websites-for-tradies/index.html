Built for how your customers actually find a tradie

People type the trade and the suburb into Google. They check the map and the reviews, then often your website. Then they ring. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence (Google Business Profile Help). Your website and profile tell Google what you do, where you work and that people rate you.

What about hipages, Airtasker and Facebook? Keep them if they bring work. You just don’t own those pages.

What every tradie website we build includes

  • Click-to-call and a quote form. A tap-to-call number on every page. A short quote form that asks for the suburb and job type.
  • A page for each main service. Decking, switchboards, hot water. Each main job gets its own page, so customers and Google find it.
  • Your service area, stated clearly. The towns you actually travel to, named once.
  • Real job photos. Your own work, sized to load fast on a phone.
  • Trust details. Licence, ABN, insurance and reviews, checked before launch.
  • Google Business Profile set-up or tidy-up. Linked to the site.
  • Search basics. Page titles, structured data, a sitemap and Search Console.

Tradie website FAQs

How much does a tradie website cost on the Mid North Coast?

Websites start from $1,250, no GST added. Most tradies who want a page per service, a job gallery and a quote form fit our Professional site, from $2,500. Care is from $49 a month. You get a fixed price in writing before work starts.

Do I need a website if I already get work from hipages, Airtasker or Facebook?

It’s worth having. Those platforms can bring in jobs, but you don’t own the page and you’re listed beside competitors. Your own website shows your real work and service area, links to your Google Business Profile and takes quote requests directly.

What should a tradie website include?

Click-to-call, a short quote form, a page for each main service, your service area, real job photos and your licence, ABN and reviews. Add a linked Google Business Profile and the search basics, and you’ve covered what customers check before they ring.

Can you set up or fix my Google Business Profile as well?

Yes. We set up new profiles or tidy existing ones, then link them to your website. Google says complete, accurate details and more positive reviews can help your local ranking, so we get the category, hours, service area and photos right.

Can my website capture missed calls or after-hours enquiries?

Yes. We can set up a text back when you miss a call, capture enquiries that come in after hours and send follow-ups when a quote goes quiet. You decide what gets sent, and we set it up and support it locally.

Can I add my own job photos after launch?

Yes. Send them through whenever you finish a job worth showing. Small text and photo changes are included in website care, which starts from $49 a month along with hosting, updates and backups. Fresh photos of your own work keep the site current.

Should I list my licence number and ABN on my website?

Yes, where your trade needs it. Customers often check before they ring, and seeing your licence and ABN builds trust. You supply the details and we check them before launch. Just make sure the ABN matches the name you trade under.

How long does it take to build a tradie website?

Typically about a week. It can take longer for bigger sites, or if photos and wording are still being sorted out. We agree the plan and price first, so you know what’s happening, when it’s happening and what it costs.

How long until my tradie website shows up on Google?

Nobody can honestly give you a date. It depends on your competition, your Google Business Profile, your reviews and how clearly the site says what you do and where. We set up the groundwork properly and keep improving it after launch.

Do you only work with tradies in Forster?

No. We’re based in Forster, but we work with tradies across Tuncurry, Taree, Wingham, Old Bar, Port Macquarie and the rest of the Mid North Coast. Most work happens by phone and email, and we meet in person for major projects.
